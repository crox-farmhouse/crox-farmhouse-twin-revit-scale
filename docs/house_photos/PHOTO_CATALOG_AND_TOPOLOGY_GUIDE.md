## Architectural & Topological Ground Truths

### 1. Levels & Floor Heights
- **Kitchen & Dining:** Level `0.00m` (reference datum).
- **Hallway / Stair Hall / Boot Lobby:** Dark slate flagstone floor at `-0.30m` (one step down from kitchen/dining).
- **Front Entrance Hallway (`IN`), Living Room & Front Room:** Situated at the lower level (`-0.60m`), with a distinct `30cm` step UP from the Front Entrance Hallway into the Stair Hall (`-0.30m`).
- **Thresholds:** Clearly marked with slate/timber step nosings rather than flush transitions.

### 2. The Staircase & Stair Hall
- **Layout:** Quarter-turn staircase. Lower flight starts on the right and rises ~5 risers Northwards to a landing, then turns East/right up to the first floor landing.
- **Balustrade:** Traditional square white painted spindles/balusters with simple square newel posts and flat cap tops.
- **Under-Stairs:** Fully enclosed with white-painted vertical tongue-and-groove boards; fitted with a small cupboard door with black latch and black surface hinges.
- **Ceiling:** Lower historic ceiling height with heavy painted timber beam and exposed joists.
- **Stair Void:** Visible from the first floor landing over the balustrade, looking directly down to the ground floor black slate flagstones.

### 3. Room Distinctions: Front Room vs. Living Room
- **Front Room (Dark Room / Cinema Room):**
  - Dark slate/charcoal grey walls (with light grey on the entrance partition wall) and white-painted ceiling beams/joists near the door.
  - Recessed fireplace with black wood burning stove and brick hearth.
  - Full-height custom built-in white cabinets on both sides of the chimney breast.
- **Living Room (Light Room):**
  - Light warm grey walls.
  - White-painted exposed wooden ceiling beams.
  - Large red brick fireplace with exposed brick arch, black hood canopy, raised fire basket on brick plinth, and **herringbone brick hearth** extending into the room.
  - Symmetrical white built-in units: open bookshelves over 4-door shaker base cupboards flanking the chimney breast (TV mounted on right unit).
  - Multi-panel splay bay window with bespoke slatted radiator cover underneath.
  - Located with the plant room behind it (external access only, no internal door to plant room).

### 4. Non-Square Historic Geometry
- As observed in Photo 12 and the user's explicit notes, walls are not strictly $90^\circ$ orthogonal.
- Door frames have deep historic reveals and wall thicknesses vary between structural stone/brick walls (~300–450mm) and internal partitions (~100–150mm).
- The 3D model should prioritize alignment with visible visual sightlines, doorways, and structural chimney stacks.

### 5. Front Entrance Hallway (`IN`) Trapezoidal Convergence & Ceiling Boxing (Photos 19–23)
- **Irregular Trapezoid Plan (Straight Walls, No Kink):** Both the North and South walls of the Front Entrance Hallway (`IN`) are completely straight from the West front entrance door to the East end wall — there is **no kink** in either wall. They converge steadily toward the East end, where the hallway narrows to approximately **60cm (`0.60m`) wide** at the blank East end wall.
- **North Wall Doorway & Step-Up Separation (Photo 19, 22):** At the East end of the North wall, the 4-panel door into the Front Room / Cinema Room and the cased opening stepping up (`+30cm`) into the Stair Hall are side-by-side, separated by a short (~25–30cm) flat wall pier with skirting board.
- **North–South Cross-Hall Sightline (Photos 21, 23):** The cased step-up opening from the Stair Hall (`Y = -0.30m`) aligns directly across the narrow East end of the Front Entrance Hallway (`Y = -0.60m`) with the Living Room doorway on the South wall, while the Front Room doorway sits immediately adjacent to the West.
- **Ceiling Bulkhead Boxing & First-Floor Level Drop (Photos 19, 20, 22):** At the narrow East end of the Front Entrance Hallway ceiling, there is a stepped plasterboard bulkhead / boxing running across the width of the hall (corresponding to the step down in the first-floor landing/corridor above), plus high-level boxing along both the North and South upper wall corners toward the West front door.
